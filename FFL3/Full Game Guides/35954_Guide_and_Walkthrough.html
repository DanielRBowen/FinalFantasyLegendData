Final Fantasy Legend III
FAQ / Walkthrough

by Spatvark
(SpatFAQs@gmail.com)

Millenia ago a fearsome battle raged between beings who sought to become the ruler of a place knownas Pureland. Unfortunately for the rest of the universe, the Purelanders were immortal. But rathr than causing them to give up the fight, this little fact actually seemed to spur them on to create new and more powerful weapons and magic attacks. The warring grew so violent that shock waves began disturbing neighboring dimensions, eroding the very beaches of time itself. The battle was spreading beyond the Puteland space-time continuum!

Pounded by these shock waves and fearing the loss of his own Universe, Sol, the Ruler of the World, built the Talon, a space-time battle cruiser with a drive system so powerful it could outrace light, and navigate the pathways separating dimensions. Commanding this weapon, Sol finally managed to seal the Pureland warriors in their own burned-out dimension--but at a terrible cost.

Exhausted after his ordeal, Sol returned to his universe with one last task to perform. He divided the internal mechanisms of the Talon into 13 Units. Using has last ounce of energy, Sol simply willed the Units to various historical times and places on the World and Pureland. Having finished his work, Sol sank into a deep, deep slumber.

Many centuries have passed, and the warriors of Pureland have done the impossible--they have actually joined forces! Their purpose? To thank Sol for butting in on their business...

A crackling thunderclap resounded through time, announcing the arrival of the Pureland Water Entity. The Entity shimmerd with protective magical fields. So potent was the Purelanders' magic, that the colossal Water Entity was instantly visible to every generation that had inhabited--or would inhabit--the World.

As the trans-dimensional Entity began to flood the World's past, present and future with putrid water, it poured forth savage monsters without number. The level of the sea rose at a frightening pace. People fled inland. Monsters systematically sacked the towns of the World, as though searching for something...

Only the Elder of Dharm, a city tightly sealed from the water, knew what the monsters were after: the legendary battle cruiser, Talon. The Units of the Talon--wherever they were--would have to be found, and installed, before Monsters could find them, and before the World was swallowed by the sea.

When the Water Entity appeared, people all over the world wondered what it was; the water that sprung forth from its maw quickly began to flood the world though. Sent from a future of devastation, three children are brought up in a world where the oceans do not rule the world. When they come of age, they set of back to the future they came from to put an end to the Water Entity once and for all...

Hardly one of the most played Final Fantasy games, Final Fantasy Legend III was actually the first RPG I ever beat and for that, it will always hold a place in my heart. Simple to play, but hard to beat, Final Fantasy Legend III offers pure, unadulterated fun and is a welcome break to the angst and misery of its more famous siblings.

Walkthrough

Part 0: Tips & Tricks

  1. Jump EVERYWHERE. It reduces the amount of time you're on the floor, and thus means you get into less random encounters.
  2. The quickest way to go down a floor is to simply jump into a hole.
  3. Enemies are incredibly easy to classify into groups, depending upon what magic they're weak against. Plants and fire-creatures are weak against Ice, sea-creatures are weak against Fire, etc. However, there are a few cases that can be confusing. Simply experiment!
  4. Upgrade your equipment CONSTANTLY. It's expensive to do so, and while not immediately apparent in your stats, your characters are virtually guaranteed to now be better fighters.
  5. Each characters health affects their speed. The higher the HP, the faster the speed. Keep this in mind for boss battles; keep your health up so you can get your hits and further healing in first!
  6. Whenever you get a supporting character, it's a good idea to level the main characters until they're the same level as the support. This is coz' the support character is set at a fixed level which should get them through the area that they're with you for, ergo, it's the level your characters should be at to survive.
  7. When selecting battle options, press Start to be able to choose Parry or Guard; you may just need them.
  8. Almost every enemy is weak to instant-kill moves such as Stone, Exit and Fatal, but bear in mind that killing an enemy with Exit will not get you any experience.
  9. While the transformation system can seem intimidating at first glance, you generally don't have to worry about being any specific sub-species. If you want to try out being a Beast or a Cyborg, go ahead and do so!

Walkthrough

Part 1: The Beginning (Present)

NOTE: This walkthrough is written with the assumption that no Transformations will be carried out on the party. This is actually the harder route for the game, transformations used correctly allow for an easier playthrough, but people can be funny like that! I've added the occasional note when transformations would be a good idea, for those who do want to use them.

We begin... in a fight! Myron will be your strongest character, so have him attack a Diviner on his own. Arthur and Curtis should attack one of the Sprites while Gloria casts Ice on the other. In the second round, just twat the remaining enemies; there should only be one Diviner left.

Once the fight is over, it turns out you were only using a Battle Simulator! Just read the text and scroll through; all the actions here are controlled by the game for a while.

When you regain control, you should be standing atop the Talon, your ticket home... if only it worked! It seems like it needs these things called Units, and Elder has already given you one! So, you now need to head to the North Tower to get the spell, Float, but first, I recommend returning to Dharm. Level up using the simulator until you're at least matching Myron's level, and stock Cure and Ice spells onto Curtis and Gloria, they'll be your main magicians. Once you've done that, leave Dharm and head directly North from it to the lake. Go around it to the right and then up to the North coast. Head West and around the mountains to the tower.

Inside the tower, head straight up the stairs on the first screen. Next, head left and down around the bend to get Shell from the chest. Head back to the stairs and go right this time to find the stairs up. Head straight left, round the back of the stairs, and open the chest from behind it. Jump over it and take the stairs up. Open the chest to find Float!

Exit the tower and cast Float immediately. You'll be sucked into an upside-down cone for some reason, but whatever. West of Dharm is Elan, a cave that goes under the ground to a hidden village! Inside, are two key places; Cronos' house at the very bottom, and Granny's magic shop, which is hidden. To find the magic shop, head into the inn and take the stairs down. Enter the spring and two squares down on the extreme left side is a secret pathway, leading to the waterfall. Simply walk up where the water is coming down and you're in the shop!

Speaking of shopping, you can buy Psi knives in the weapons shop, which are great weapon for your mutants, Curtis and Gloria. It's also pretty good on Arthur and Sharon, but your Mutants will be using these for quite a good while so it's well worth going out of your way to buy two of these now.

In Cronos' house, talk to the guy in the baggy robe and he'll give you the Unit that allows the Talon to travel to the Past. After getting new equipment, weapons and spells, leave Elan and cast Float again. Fly to where the Talon is being kept and you'll discover a Waterhag is blocking the way!

Part 2: Back in Time (Past)

Head North-East of the Palace, and you'll find a strangely familiar-looking old man there. Talk to him and you'll discover he's a past version of Dharm's Elder! Cast Float and head South-West from the Palace to the village of Lae. Head straight up from the entrance to find an item shop. Talk to the shopkeep, and it'll give you the Flushex Unit. After grabbing Fire1 and Aero spells from the magic shop, leave and cast Float again.

NOTE: Lae also sells the Punch Martial Arts Skill, which marks the first effective Talent that Beasts can use, making them a useful party member if you do want to use Transformations. Turning Sharon into a Beast at this point can be very helpful, especially into high Agility types, making sure she has learnt Cure1.

Go North to Elan and head straight for Cronos' house. He's a little kid now, but he tells you that Granny has gone off to save Lara in the cave, which you'll find South of the Palace, but you don't want to do that. Trust me, unless you've been levelling like mad, you WILL get slaughtered. Level up to about level 10 and then venture inside (though, having said that, I went and did my levelling on the first floor of the cave anyway =P).

Inside, take the left path and follow it around to find a Plume. Return to the entrance and take the right path this time. It'll take you to Granny; she'll say Lara was taken by Dogra and then shuffle back to Elan. Don't go down the stairs, but go to the left instead. The path will take you to a chest containing Iron Nunchucks. Now, return to the stairs and down we go!

Head right and then go all the way up to the top. Go left and take the stairs down. Follow the path to the right and down. Take the first turn to the left and take the stairs up. Follow the stairs and you'll find Lara...

Part 4: To the Future! (Future)

Once you've left the Palace, cast Float and head West to Elan. Talk to Cronos and then talk to Granny, who'll give you the Morph spell, before stocking up on the new wares, including the aforementioned Gold Shields. Head for Muu, which is still beneath the sea, North of Elan. When you enter, cast the Morph spell so you take the appearance of a Waterhag, and thus can converse with the locals. Buy four Psi Swords for your entire party (these will be Curtis and Gloria's weapons for a good while, but is also the best choice for Arthur and Sharon at the moment if they're both still Human) and get Quake and Ice2 spells for Curtis and Gloria. Get any other equip you need; you may want to take this time to earn a lot of GP since stuff's starting to get really expensive, and the fights in the future are far tougher as well, so you'll need the levels.

South-East from Elan (or an easier way to find it is to go South from the Palace until you reach a tower, and then head due West) you'll find a graveyard, in the following formation:

Use the circled tombstone to uncover a passageway leading to New Dharm. Go into the Northern-most building and talk to Myron; he'll tell you to go and talk to Dion in Viper, giving you the password to get into the headquarters there: "ERAUQS"

Head due North to Viper and get the Dragon Armour and Shields for everyone, excepting the person with the Psi Armour, before taking the right-hand tram and talking to the guard. Enter the building and talk to Dion, he'll give you the Rocket, and join the party, telling you to talk to Dr. Pulcer. Open the chests in the bottom-right corner for some goodies, including the awesome Berth Unit, which is basically a free inn!

Leave Viper and dive underwater, heading North to the Undersea Shelter. Talk to Dr. Pulcer and he'll give you the Radio, and inform you of the plan to get to Floatland so you can defeat Maitreya and get the X-Plane Unit needed to get to Pureland. Make sure to grab the Cure3 potion before leaving.

Make sure to check out any sunken ships you haven't already to get extra goodies. Once you've done that, head back to the Palace, and take the Northern-most position on the pier as you can before using the Rocket to get to Floatland.

Part 5: Traversing Floatland (Floatland)

Enter the village and stock up on the Cure3 and Fire2 magic, as well as some new armour as well. When you're ready, head North to the Northern Ruins. Inside, note the cloud-covered segments of floor; these work as conveyor belts like in Chaos' Castle, but you can't tell which way these will take you without actually getting on them.

Make your way to the right-hand side and walk through the wall there. Use the cloud-belts to be carried South to a warp, but before using it, step on the cloud-belt to the left to be taken to a chest containing an Earth Stone, and then step once more upon the left-hand cloud-belt. This should take you back to the room you started in, so work your way to the warp once more and use it this time.

Jump over the cloud-belts to go up two squares, and then one to the left. You should be able to see a chest within the wall to your left; from the top-left corner of the square you're standing on, head straight left to find another hidden room, containing a Fire Bomb within the chest. Leave the small room and you should come out right next to the warp to the next floor.

In the bottom-left corner is another wall you can walk through, go into the warp there and follow the path to find an X-Fire Staff, before returning to the warp and opening the chest there to receive a Water Stone. Leave via the other passageway and go to the warp to get to the fourth floor.

Head down to get a Water Stone and then make your way West to the stairs to the next floor. Open the chest to get an SMG Gun before heading left to get the Remote. Go back to the warp, but instead of using it, go directly South to find another hidden room, leading to a staircase.

On the final floor, head up and then left to get a Light Stone, and then take the stairs back to the first floor. Get the ATM Cannon and then take the door to find yourself in the center of the first chamber. Head straight upwards to leave, and then head back to the village to heal and stock up on anything you might need.

Once you've done that, head back towards the Northern Ruins, but continue past it, following the path until you reach a large set of mountains. Go up the first ladder, and then take the path to the right. Follow it around to find an Ice Shield before returning to the first ladder. This time, take the left-hand ladder, and when you get the choice, take the ladder downwards. Follow the path around to get an Air Stone, and then return to the ladder you came down, going up the ladder you previously ignored. Follow the path around until you reach Maitreya's Tower.

Head straight upwards to the second floor, and then circle to the right to get a Cure3 potion, and then head up to the third floor. Note how it looks exactly the same as the second floor; floors two to eight are all identical, except for chest placement. It is as follows:

  • Floor 2: Right (Cure 3 Potion)
  • Floor 3: None
  • Floor 4: Left (Psi Gun; equip this on Curtis or Gloria)
  • Floor 5: None
  • Floor 6: Right (Earth Stone)
  • Floor 7: None
  • Floor 8: Right (Exit)

On floor nine, talk to the monster and you'll all fall down to the eighth floor, except for Dion. He'll uncover a secret door leading to Maitreya...

Once you've gained control of the party again, head up to the ninth floor, but ignore the secret door, instead going up to the tenth floor, where you'll find a Cure3 potion and Battle Armour. Go back down to the ninth floor and now use the secret door in the top-right corner. Follow the path around until you reach Maitreya.

Part 6: Welcome to the Pureland! (Pureland)

Upon arriving, Dion will leave the party to try and find the advance party. Take Talon to the town just East of you, Darius, and make sure to grab the Star Bomb and Heal spell, located in chests. Stock up on new armour, especially Diamond Shields for everyone, though keep the Dragon Shoes for Curtis and Gloria, since it gives more magic. Also, buy a second Psi Gun and equip it on the Mutant who doesn't currently have one. Once you're done there, leave Darius and get back aboard Talon and head West to a small village.

Enter the house to the left and talk to Eitar, he'll tell you that Faye was taken to the dungeon to the North, but everyone in the town has been turned to stone by the monster in there. He also suggests getting some catnip from the tree to put the guards to sleep. Leave the house and loop around the top towards the West and into the small field. Go over the broken fence and grab some Catnip before leaving the town and heading North.

When you enter the dungeon, use the Catnip, and then head left. Follow the path along and stick to the outside to get to a chest containing Wizard Armour. Head right until you reach a staircase that's two cells wide, and then head up it to find the boss.

Talents

Talents are abilities innate to sub-species, rather than spells bought from a shop or cast from using an item. Humans and Mutants are not able to use Talents, but the other four species, Beast, Monster, Cyborg, and Robot all can.

Some Talents can both be used as an attack, and also as a counterattack. When a Talent is used as a counterattack, it does half damage.

Talents can target either a single enemy, a group of enemies, or all enemies. For Talents that target a group, damage is divided by the number of targets. For Talents that target either a single or all enemies will instead do full damage.

The formula for damage dealt is as follows:

Damage = SpeciesSkill(25% * TalentPower(Attack + (MaxHP / 10)) + BaseDamage) - TargetDefence

SpeciesSkill is always 1, unless it is a Robot using a Robot Talent, which instead has a SpeciesSkill of 1.5.

Transformations

NOTE: This is not a part of the game I am especially knowledgeable about. However, since Devin Morgan removed his FFL3 FAQ from GameFAQs, he has allowed me to adapt parts of it for this FAQ, including this section on transformations.

In the universe of FFL3, there are six different Species: Humans, Mutants, Beasts, Monsters, Cyborgs and Robots. Each have their own strengths and weaknesses, and any of the party members can transform into any of the six Species.

Humans

One of the starting species, Humans can initially seem quite powerful with their double damage with most weapons compared to the other species. Unfortunately though, their HP lags behind Monsters and Beasts, their MP is far too poor for repeated casting of healing, and Beasts' damage multiplier with Martial Arts Skills puts them on an equivalent level when it comes to damage output. Furthermore, once the Mystic Swords come into play, all classes that can equip weapons can reach the same level of damage.

The one true advantage Humans have is the ability to exceed 999 HP and MP, but this is only reachable at really high levels, so they lag behind the other species (except for Mutants) for most of the game.

Mutants

The other starting species, people like to write Mutants off as bad since they're only good with magic, and as the enemy numbers in battles are smaller than the previous Legend / SaGa games, magic is often not that useful in comparison to just whacking things with other species.

Personally, I just don't find this to be true: their double skill with Magic means they're much more likely to be about to oneshot enemies and even groups, and the double skill with Psi weapons actually becomes very handy when trying to save MP, espeically the Psi Guns found later in the game.

Mutants' generally lower HP means they're rather squishy through most of the game, but like Humans, they can actually exceed the 999 HP and MP caps. Since this requires incredibly high levels to overtake the other species though, their lack of health is always going to be an issue.

Beasts

Beasts are very bad at using (most) weapons, except for the Martial Arts Skills (and going unarmed / using the Mystic Swords), but can match Humans in physical damage, even hitting as hard as Mystic Swords with the top tier Martial Arts. Once Mystic Swords do become available, it may help to switch to them as while the damage output is generally the same, Martial Arts Skills do not bypass physical resistances, which weakens them against later bosses. Their talents can vary quite a lot, but they usually have useful status abilities and insta-kill attacks, meaning they can do a bit of everything pretty well.

Beasts will change form automatically by levelling up, including every level once they reach the final tier, swapping between Anubis and Aeshma, which is unfortunate as Anubis is vastly superior. Having two Beasts, one of each form would help ensure there's always at least one Anubis in your party at that point, which will help a lot.

Monsters

Monsters are unable to use equipment, but counteract this with high stats, great evade, and an absolute ton of MP. This makes them great at both healing and tanking, so it makes sense to have at least one in your party for most of the game. However, by the final dungeon, their lack of elemental and status resistance turns them into a a huge liability, so at this point they should be converted into Beasts instead. Highlights are Baby-D, GigaWorm, Remora, and Wheel.

Unlike Beasts, Monsters do not change forms upon levelling up, they require eating more meat to transform into a stronger Monster once they have reached the next level threshold.

Cyborgs

Cyborgs are a strange one, in that you can actually make one of your party into a really strong Cyborg by end game, but trying to use more than one is a terrible mistake. They only keep 40% of their HP and MP, 70% of the Attack, Agility and Magic, and 20% of their Defence when compared to the enemy version of the species, and try to make up this loss with the equipment that they're using. However, you only really have the appropriate gear to fit out one of them properly by endgame meaning that until the Mystic Swords start becoming available, it's not really worth using this species.

Robots

It is easy to write Robots off as the worst species in the game when you first start using them, as they seem incredibly weak. only keeping 20% of the HP, 0% of the MP and Magic, 50% of the Attack and Agility, and 10% of the Defence of the original enemy version of the species. However, once you start getting more money, the Capsules that Robots use to increase their stats become more affordable, allowing the player to farm and max out at 999 HP and 99 Attack, Defence, and Agility (plus the Robot's base stats). This makes them great tanks at end game, albeit since they have no MP, they cannot cast any magic apart from costless spells (Dive, Float, and Item Magic).

Unlike Cyborgs, Robots do not change forms upon levelling up, they require installing more parts to transform into a stronger Robot once they have reached the next level threshold. This means that if you stumble upon a Robot species you really like, you can keep them as they are without worrying about enforced changes upon level up.

To change into the different Species, a character must either eat the Meat or install the Parts discovered after winning a random battle. What effect these Meats and Parts will have is determined by what Species the character currently is, and what species the Meat or Part that is causing the change is from.
